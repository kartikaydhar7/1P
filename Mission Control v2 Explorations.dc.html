Today's Mission Control is a vertical scroll — brief, then queue, then ambient. You read it top to bottom and act at the end. A command centre should show state and let you act without scrolling. Three ways to do that, each borrowing structure from a different one of the design skills you gave me. All three stay on Evergreen so they don't clash with the rest of the app.

1a The Console from the dashboard skill — data-dense grid, flat surfaces, 8px rhythm, nothing below the fold

Three fixed panes. The queue on the left is the only thing that scrolls; agent state and the numbers are always visible on the right. You never lose sight of what's running while you work an item.

1b The Triage Inbox from the agentic skill — one stream of decisions, keyboard-first, zero chrome

Mission Control as an inbox you empty. Every item is a decision Paula has already prepared; you move with J/K, approve with E, and the count goes down. When it hits zero you're done for the day — that's the whole interface.

1c The Situation Board from the editorial skill — front-page hierarchy, hairline rules, serif lead

Your morning as a front page. One lead story sized like a headline, secondary columns for the rest, hairline rules instead of cards. Calm, scannable in three seconds, and it makes the stakes feel real rather than administrative.
